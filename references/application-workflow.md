# Application workflow

## Candidate evidence

Record each material claim with provenance: candidate-confirmed, résumé, verified project/repository, employer record, or unknown. Project descriptions should specify the candidate's actual contribution and current status. Treat public profile content as evidence of existence, not proof of authorship or results. Do not convert “used a tool” into “expert”, “built a server”, “led a team”, or “shipped to production”. Never invent metrics, references, salaries, dates, legal status or assessment results. Preserve the preferred original résumé when requested; tailoring must remain factual and reviewable. An ATS score is an estimate unless obtained from a named, verifiable service.

## Search and selection

1. Translate preferences into role families, location and eligibility filters. Explore synonyms and adjacent roles only within the candidate's stated scope.
2. Open the actual posting where possible and check title, employer, location, current status, eligibility, required experience and application path. Label unverified details. Prioritize fit and avoid suspicious or stale listings.
3. Normalize employer, role, location and posting URL. Check existing tracker and sent mail where authorized before applying, including alternate URLs and reused postings. Keep separate requisitions distinct only when supported by evidence.
4. Tailor the summary and answers to the actual description. Explain transferable experience honestly for junior consulting or training roles; do not imply prior formal tenure. Personal opinion prompts require either candidate input or clearly proposed wording the user has authorized you to draft.

## Submission and records

Track discovery, preparation, submission and response separately. Record posting URL, company, role, location, source, status, dates, evidence, next step and notes. A `submitted` record requires a confirmation page, confirmation email, sent application email or equivalent direct evidence. A filled form, click, optimistic toast, pending verification or draft is not proof. Record a bounce against the original attempt and do not count it as a delivered application unless delivery is independently established. Reconcile uncertainty before retrying. A daily number is unique confirmed submissions, not jobs found, forms opened or retries.

Respect user instructions on résumé, contact, work hours, compensation, notice, privacy and site access. External messages require authorization. Summarize consequential replies and invitations; do not promise an interview time or commit to an agreement without authority. Do not forward private data to unrelated services.

## Check replies and follow up

On each job-search session or explicit inbox check, search the connected mailbox for recent recruiter and employer messages, then compare them with the application tracker and sent-mail threads. A practical Gmail starting query is `newer_than:14d {"application" "interview" "assessment" "recruiter"}`; also search by employer name or sender for tracked applications, since replies may have generic subjects. Search the inbox and spam only when a reply seems missing, and inspect the full thread before acting.

For each relevant message, record the received date, sender, company/role, a concise outcome, and the next action with any deadline. Classify it as an acknowledgment, screening question, interview or assessment invitation, rejection, offer, delivery failure, or unrelated message. Surface time-sensitive invitations and deadlines first. A receipt acknowledgment is not a hiring decision. Update the tracker without changing an application to `submitted` merely because a recruiter replied.

Answer routine screening questions only from confirmed profile facts and within the user's existing authorization. If a reply requires new personal facts, a schedule commitment, salary negotiation, contract acceptance, or another decision the user has not authorized, prepare a concise draft and ask the user. Never accept an offer or confirm an interview time on the user's behalf without clear authorization. If the user asks only for an inbox check, summarize and update records; do not send a follow-up.

For interviews and assessments, record the date, time zone, deadline, format, duration, and required candidate action. Flag time-sensitive windows first. The candidate must personally complete proctored interviews and assessments; never impersonate them or submit test work as their own. Treat a retake or new assessment as optional unless the candidate chooses to proceed. Keep passwords, one-time codes, and private assessment credentials out of public files, trackers, and chat summaries; tell the candidate when an email contains a credential so they can handle it securely. Verify unusual assessment links or sender claims before recommending that the candidate sign in.

When no response arrives, follow the user's stated follow-up cadence. If none is set, do not send repeated nudges automatically: record a suggested follow-up date and ask before sending the first one. Check for an existing reply immediately before any follow-up to avoid duplicate or poorly timed messages.
